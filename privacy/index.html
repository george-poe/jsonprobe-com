Privacy

What happens to your data on this site, stated precisely — including the one place where something does leave your browser.

Last updated 2026-09-13. This page describes the site as it is built. If a sentence here stops matching the code, the sentence is the bug — say so and it gets fixed.

Your document is not transmitted to us

Formatting, minifying, validating, diffing, the tree view and the JSONPath query all run on your machine. There is no upload endpoint, no backend, no account system and no database behind this site. The page you are reading is a static file.

The processing happens in a Web Worker inside this tab (`assets/worker.js`, loading `assets/engine.js`). Worker messages never cross the origin, so no request made by the tools carries your document anywhere. The engine source ships with the page: open `view-source:https://jsonprobe.com/assets/engine.js` and search for `fetch`, `XMLHttpRequest` or `sendBeacon` — there is one `fetch`, described below, and it is not in the formatting path.

The one exception: schema by URL

On the Schema tab you can either paste a schema or name a URL for one. If you choose the URL, your browser requests that URL from its own host. We are not in the middle of that: the request carries no copy of your document, only the address you typed, and we never see it or its response.

If you do not want that request to exist, paste the schema instead. Nothing else on the site fetches anything.

Who does see something

These files are delivered by GitHub Pages from an IP in `185.199.108.0/22`. Like every web server, GitHub's edge records the standard request line: your IP address, user agent string, the path requested, a timestamp and the `Referer` header if your browser sent one. That log exists whether or not you paste anything into a tool, because requesting the page is what makes the page appear.

If you open a page through a search engine, the search engine also knows you asked. That is on their side, not ours.

Ads and consent

This site is served with Google AdSense enabled. Adverts come from `pagead2.googlesyndication.com` and `googleadservices.com`, so your browser talks to Google when you load a page here — that is a third-party request, and it is visible in a network panel. We have reworded every sentence that would stop being true, rather than keep it.

Google uses cookies to serve ads based on your prior visits to this or other sites. The Google advertising cookie lets Google and its partners use data for ad targeting. You can opt out at https://www.google.com/settings/ads or at https://www.aboutads.info/choices, and Google's own policy on what it collects is at https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites.

In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland you should see a consent message before any personalised advertising. If you do not see one, your region may not require it here, or the message was dismissed; either way the amount of data shared with Google is governed by that consent choice, not by anything on this site.

Revenue from ads does not change what the tools do with your input: nothing about the formatting path moves because an advert is on the page.

Analytics

This build ships no analytics of any kind; if one is ever added, the request shows up in your own network panel and this paragraph gets rewritten. Nothing measures which documents you format, what you search for, or how long you stay — a page counter would need a request to whoever serves it, and this site makes none for that purpose. If a network panel on a page here shows nothing but the page itself and its own files, that is the whole story.

Cookies and storage

This site sets no cookies of its own and keeps nothing in `localStorage` or `IndexedDB`. Your input is not remembered if you reload: it stays in a textarea until the tab goes away. With advertising enabled, Google may set cookies on its own domains; those are theirs to read, not ours.

Contact

Mistakes, or something on this page that no longer matches reality: open an issue on https://github.com/george-poe/jsonprobe-com. The repository is where the pages and their tests are built, so a report lands next to the code that would have to change. There is no email list, and no inquiry here is answered by anyone but the person who builds the site.

Every tool on this site runs inside your browser tab, and nothing you paste is sent anywhere. The one outbound request the code can make is the optional schema-by-URL fetch on the Schema tab: it sends the URL you typed, never your document.
